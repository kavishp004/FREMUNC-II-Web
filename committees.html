United Nations Human Rights Council

Topic: Protecting Neural Data and Human Rights in the Age of Brain Neurotechnology

Type: GA Advanced

Chairs: Nitya Misra & Kshitij Markandeya

Contact: mun.irvington@gmail.com

As neurotechnology becomes more advanced, the ability to access information directly from the brain is challenging traditional ideas of privacy. Existing laws were created before this technology was widely available, leaving governments to determine how human rights should apply in a world where a person’s brain activity can become a source of data. As these technologies become more common, the lack of clear international rules has created growing concern over how far their use should be allowed to go. The issue now centers on finding a balance between technological progress and protecting people from potential misuse.

United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs

Topic: Developing an International Framework for Lunar Resource Extraction

Type: GA Beginner

Chairs: Sahil Markandeya & Naisha Vora

Contact: mun.irvington@gmail.com

While our discoveries in space are evolving over time, lunar mining has shifted to an urgent reality. As we continue to find rare and essential resources like water and metals on the Moon, it is quickly becoming a profitable market for dominant nations and private companies, risking its environmental protection and critical research. Outdated and vague space laws have made major legal unclarity regarding the ownership and rights of the land and resources. Without clear guidelines, conflict will continue to risk permanent environmental damage and unequal access for various nations. In UNOOSA, delegates will collaborate and work to find a balanced framework to protect lunar environments and still allow innovation with equal access.

Historical Crisis

Topic: The Decision to Use the Atomic Bomb: Hiroshima and Nagasaki, 1945

Type: Crisis Historical

Chairs: Rayan Attari & Pradyun Adababla

Contact: mun.irvington@gmail.com

Germany has surrendered in WWII but the war hasn’t ended yet. Japan is unwilling to surrender and the Soviet Union is preparing an attack against them. During this time the United States has successfully tested a new atomic weapon which could lead to devastating impact. Delegates will change the fate of the world as they decide whether Japan should surrender, if the United States should use the bomb, and how much influence the Soviet Union will gain on Asia. These choices will not only determine how the war ends but will affect the future of the world.

Crisis

Topic: The Avengers Initiative: Defending Earth from Global Threats

Type: Crisis

Chairs: Riya Vyas & Harry Stephenson

Contact: mun.irvington@gmail.com

For decades, the Spirits of Vengeance have hunted only the truly guilty — until now. Something has gone wrong. The original Ghost Rider has vanished, and ordinary people across the globe are being bonded with spirits, creating new, uncontrolled riders. No one is safe from the hellfire: not the corrupt, not the careless, and possibly not even the Avengers themselves. Delegates will step into the roles of Earth’s mightiest heroes, racing to track down the Riders, uncover the truth, and decide how far they’re willing to go to stop a force of justice that’s spiraling out of control.

Specialized Committee

Topic: Escalating Tensions Between the United States and Iran

Type: Spec

Chairs: Elina Mangla & Shaan Sood

Contact: mun.irvington@gmail.com

The United States and Iran are at war. It is up to the international community to secure peace and find a solution to the active conflict. In this GA-style committee, delegates will represent countries key to the conflict, hoping to find a solution that will bring stability to the region. Delegates must take into account the deeper political, social, and economic conditions that led the two nations to war. Every resolution will demand diplomacy and compromise to restore peace within the Middle East.
